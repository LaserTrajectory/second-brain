## 📓 Notes & Annotations
> Comparing the evolution of an aggregate outcome (e.g., state-level crime rate) between  a unit affected by the event or intervention of interest and a set of unaffected units requires  only aggregate data, which are often available. However, when data are not available  at the same level of aggregation as the outcome of interest, information on a sample of  disaggregated units can sometimes be used to estimate the aggregate outcomes of interest  (like in Card, 1990, and Card and Krueger, 1994).1 [p. 1](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/I699XSN6?page=1&annotation=C5IES9B9)
- **My Note:** Card and Krueger's paper is used as a motivation for situations where data for the outcome of interest is not available on the same level of aggregation as required.
