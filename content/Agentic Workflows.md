# Expanding my Skillset

**Sunday, December 14, 2025**

Part of my skillset expansion over the next couple of years is going to be learning how to code along with AI, in a way that it augments my ability to code, rather than replacing it entirely.

This requires, in my view, a measured approach to coding. For better or for worse, my AI usage in coding has been relatively limited up until now. I still am used to searching for niche fixes on StackOverflow or Github bug threads (which I think probably helped build a solid foundation).

But in today's world it would be ideal to have a workflow that allows for speed, and also allows for me to deal with frameworks and even languages I'm not familiar with, and build at speed. I'd definitely like to see where my ideas take me.

Some useful advice has been from Scott Cunningham's article on [using Claude Code as an economist](https://causalinf.substack.com/p/claude-code-changed-how-i-work-part). 

> My point is that I notice I like something only when I _do it_, not when I _say it_. And with Claude Code, I immediately noticed I was _doing it_. In fact, I was switching to doing it, and immediately adopting workflows and practices that made me convinced I was _probably never going to go back_ to the old way.

This phrase from Scott's article certainly stuck with me, and it's something I'd like to emulate myself. It's especially reassuring to see an economist use this in his tech stack and not find it to be a crutch, but rather a tool to expand his own way of thinking.

That being said, it's valuable to also think about coding best practices that are maybe "softer", or more malleable to human tendencies with respect to continuous improvement. This links in some respects to a central idea regarding [[Dynamic Policy Feedback Loops]] that hopefully eventually features in my [[thesis]].

For instance, an example of such coding best practices could be the kinds described in [this article from Github's blog](https://github.blog/news-insights/policy-news-and-insights/the-future-of-ai-powered-software-optimization-and-how-it-can-help-your-team/). It's written primarily for devs looking to streamline their workflows with agents, but I think in general its a principle that can be more generally applied to the kinds of systems I hope to build.
