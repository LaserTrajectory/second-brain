# [Lant Pritchett](https://lantpritchett.org/)

> The way in which, and the reason, I am a “critic” of RCTs is that they have never proved particularly useful in answering any of the important questions I have had in my career in a practical and definitive way. While the _randomistas_ are correct (enough) about one narrow methodological question of creating unbiased estimates of the causal impact of specific interventions there are two big problems. One, the issues of external validity and construct validity are, in development economics, just empirically huge and make the results of any specific RCT just one piece of information in any context specific decision and relying on RCT results can be demonstrated to lead to worse decisions that using context specific but weaker methods ([here](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/rode.13037)). Two, the methods of RCT require a randomized separation of units into “treatment” and “control” and hence are mainly useful in answering questions for which the individual (or household) is the relevant unit, but the most important questions about development are not reducible to individuals.

--- <cite>Lant Pritchett, "Introduction to the Development Economist", September 2025</cite> ([Substack](https://substack.com/home/post/p-173283687))

- Professor Pritchett's work on development economics is legendary. I think there are two main areas where his input/advice may be extremely useful to me: his work on [[state capacity]], and his work on basic education, which connects to my interest in [[education policy]].
- Professor Pritchett is currently at the LSE – he could be reached out to in a scenario where I would need some input from a theoretical [[development economics]] perspective.
