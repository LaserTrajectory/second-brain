# 💡 Treatment two

> [!abstract] Core Assertion
> *Empirical econometrics research has long grappled with the question of assessing treatment when certain units are not obviously affected, or when adequate counterfactuals are not present.*

## The Argument
*Logic, assertion, nuances

Econometrics in the last 30 years has had a unique focus on the treatment of unobserved or unaffected units. This involves any analysis of treatment where the direct or obvious counterfactual is not available, and therefore a facsimile must be made.

This approach, to some extent, is motivated by older studies that use clever aggregations of data at treatment boundaries to assess treatment affects. For instance, Card and Krueger (1993) use the proportionate increase in wages required to meet mandatory minimum wage requirements in New Jersey fast food stores as an aggregation – this is then used as an axis of comparison against similar stores in Pennsylvania, where no such minimum wage requirement was introduced.
